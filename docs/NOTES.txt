A1. LARAVEL "localhost" vs "127.0.0.1"                     [FIXED, but re-check]
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Symptom : You start both backends, the agent service works, Laravel does not.
          Submitting a situation report fails at the very first step.
Cause   : `localhost` resolves to IPv6 ::1 first on Windows. Laravel (php artisan
          serve) binds to IPv4 127.0.0.1 only. The browser tries ::1 and fails.
Fix     : dashboard/.env → VITE_LARAVEL_BASE_URL=http://127.0.0.1:8000
Verify  : Open the dashboard, browser console, run:  window.EMMA.probe()
          Both rows must say reachable=yes. Takes five seconds.

          ^ DO THIS BEFORE YOU GO ON STAGE. The health banner in the header only
            checks the agent service (:8001). A dead Laravel stays invisible
            until someone clicks Submit, which is the worst time to find out.


A2. WEATHER OVERLAY MAY LOOK EMPTY                       [RESOLVED 2026-08-08]
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Was: default layer `precipitation_new` only paints where rain is actually
falling, so on a clear day over Cebu the map looked like a plain street map.
Compounded by pale OWM tiles over a full-colour OSM basemap.

Rob's call: show something rather than just a map — pick the most visually
striking option.

FIXED, by testing all four layers against two basemaps and comparing:
  · Basemap  → CartoDB Dark Matter. This was the real fix. OWM tiles are pale
               and semi-transparent; on a light basemap they wash out, on dark
               they glow. Same reason every radar and ops weather display is
               dark. It is a map surface, not UI chrome, so it does not
               introduce a second design language.
  · Layer    → defaults to Clouds, not Rain. Cloud cover exists essentially
               always, and on dark it renders as dramatic banded structure that
               reads instantly as a satellite view.
  · Scope    → REVERSED IN PHASE 4. Now defaults to Alcoy and the toggle walks
               OUTWARD: Alcoy → Philippines → ASEAN, mirroring the escalation
               chain. Opening wide and flying in was top-down and fought the
               bottom-to-top narrative. At Alcoy the overlay drops to 0.15
               opacity because it carries no information at that zoom, and the
               evacuation pins carry the view instead — the legend says so.
  · Opacity  → 0.6 to 0.8 at regional scope, 0.15 at local.
  · Order    → layers are listed by how reliably they show SOMETHING:
               Clouds, Temp, Wind, Rain. Temp never renders blank at all, so it
               is the guaranteed fallback if clouds ever disappoint.

What I found testing the others, so you do not have to repeat it:
  Temp on light  — flat tan wash; the tropics are uniformly warm, so no structure
  Wind on light  — pastel purple, some structure, still weak
  Wind on dark   — muddy purple-grey, worse than clouds
  Clouds on dark — clearly the winner

ON YOUR POINT ABOUT LOCAL ZOOM: you are right and it cannot be fixed. OWM tiles
are a coarse global raster; past roughly zoom 8 one tile covers the viewport, so
it renders as flat colour with no structure. That is a property of the free tile
service, not our code.

The division that actually works for the demo:
  wide scope  (Philippines / ASEAN) → the WEATHER is the story
  local scope (Alcoy)               → the PINS are the story, evacuation centres
                                      colour-coded by occupancy, red = 90%+ full
Alcoy sits at zoom 10 rather than 11 to retain a little structure.

Suggested narration beat: open on Philippines with the cloud field, then hit
"Alcoy" — the map flies in and the story narrows from the region to the barangay
in one gesture. That is your whole local-to-regional narrative in three seconds.


A3. THE PIPELINE NOW TAKES ~30 SECONDS              [⚠ GOT WORSE IN PHASE 4]
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Measured, both on real runs against the live service:

    short hand-typed report (~350 chars) ....... 16.4s
    auto-composed SITREP  (1,092 chars) ........ 29.9s

Phase 4a made the demo path use the auto-composed SITREP, so 29.9s is now the
number that matters. Against a 45-second ASEAN segment that is TWO THIRDS of the
segment spent watching skeletons. This is the biggest open demo risk.

The cause is confirmed, not guessed: report length drives latency, because every
one of the five agents processes the full text. The Phase 4 SITREP is roughly 3x
longer than the report I originally timed, and the runtime scaled with it.

Options, cheapest first:
  (a) Pre-submit before the segment starts, present an already-populated screen.
      Costs nothing, removes the risk entirely, and the escalation beat still
      works — you click Escalate, the SITREP appears prefilled, and you cut to
      the processed view.
  (b) Trim the SITREP. It is editable in the textarea, so the presenter can cut
      it live, or I can shorten composeSitrepText() in escalation.js. Every
      anchor figure has to survive the cut or the invariants break. Say the word.
  (c) Narrate over the loading state. The staggered skeletons genuinely look
      good and reinforce "five agents working" — but 30s is a long silence.

I did NOT shorten it unilaterally, because the length is what makes it read as a
real institutional document rather than a demo string, and that trade is yours.

Also note: agent cards stagger in over ~1250ms AFTER results arrive. Do not cut
away the instant the gate appears — the later cards are still animating.


A6. PRE-SUBMITTING DOES NOT SURVIVE A ROLE SWITCH        [⚠ AFFECTS YOUR A3 PLAN]
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
You chose A3 option (a): pre-submit before the segment, present an already
populated screen. I tested whether that actually works. It does not, as-is.

App.jsx renders the active view with key={roleId}, and signing out renders the
login screen instead. So leaving the ASEAN view UNMOUNTS it — pipeline results,
report text, human-gate state, all gone. Verified directly: typed a marker into
the ASEAN textarea, switched to LGU, switched back, field was empty.

So "process it early, then come back to it later in the demo" silently resets,
and you would discover that on stage.

Three ways out:

  (i)  TWO BROWSER WINDOWS — zero code, zero risk. Park window A on the ASEAN
       view with the pipeline already processed. Run the DRRMO → DSWD → LGU walk
       in window B, click Submit SITREP to show the prefill and provenance, then
       alt-tab to window A for the processed result. Recommended this close to
       submission.

  (ii) LIFT THE PIPELINE STATE into App.jsx so the ASEAN view survives role
       switches. Perhaps 20-30 minutes. It touches AseanView, which has been
       stable since Phase 1, so it carries some regression risk right before a
       deadline. Ask if you want it — it is the clean fix, just not the safe one
       three days out.

  (iii) DO IT LIVE and narrate over the ~30s. The staggered skeletons look good
       and reinforce "five agents working", but it is a long silence.

Related, same cause: a browser REFRESH resets everything — visited seats, the
escalation payload, pipeline results. Do not hit F5 mid-demo.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
All cleared in Phase 4e. `npx eslint src/` now reports ZERO errors and ZERO

  AgentCard.jsx:82       'meta' unused ................. removed

ONE OF THESE WAS NOT COSMETIC. The `agentStates` dependency warning was hiding a
real defect: the reveal effect read agentStates but did not depend on it, and it
created setTimeout timers with no cleanup. Adding the dependency would have
restacked timers on every reveal; the fix was to schedule off the AGENTS constant
instead, and to clear the timers on unmount. That removes an actual leak — the
old code left up to six timers running if you navigated away mid-pipeline.

D1. MOCK DATA CANNOT SILENTLY DRIFT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
src/data/mockData.js derives every headline total from SCENARIO rather than
repeating numbers. validateMockData() runs 12 cross-view invariants on import in
dev and warns in the browser console if the story stops reconciling.

It has already caught two real drifts during the build (a stale "2 fund requests
· P1.295M" string, and two peso formatters disagreeing on the same figure).

If you edit the numbers, WATCH THE CONSOLE. A red [EMMA mockData] warning means
a judge could catch you.

Anchor figures, for reference:
  340 residents · 87 families · Brgy. Nug-as, Alcoy, Cebu · Typhoon Kalmaegi
  2 pregnant · 1 PWD (wheelchair) · 4 elderly 75+ · 3 infants
  Active EC occupancy sums to exactly 340 against 700 capacity
  Registry: 93 submitted = 87 validated + 3 duplicates + 3 pending


D2. GOING LIVE FOR A LOCAL VIEW IS A ONE-FUNCTION CHANGE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
No view imports mockData.js. They all go through src/data/dataSource.js, whose
getters are already async and already return API-shaped responses. To make DSWD
live, replace the body of getDswdData() with a fetch that returns the same
shape. No view changes, no hook changes — the loading and error branches are
already written and wired.

Hand dataSource.js to whoever writes the backend endpoints. Those return shapes
ARE the contract.

There is also a VITE_DATA_SOURCE env flag reserved for per-view cutover.


D3. BACKEND DEBUGGING TOOLS THAT EXIST NOW
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  window.EMMA.probe()   pings both services, prints a table (see A1)
  window.EMMA.urls      shows which URLs the dashboard is actually using
  Console logging       every request/response is logged with service tag and
                        timing; set VITE_DEBUG_API=false to silence
  Error messages        name the service, the URL and the likely fix, e.g.
                        "cannot reach agents at http://localhost:8001/health —

  Timeouts              15s Laravel, 120s agents
